# Recommend

Help the user choose by comparing viable alternatives against their goal,
constraints, and priorities. Respect settled decisions and previously rejected
options. This invocation produces advice; implementing the choice is a separate
action within the user's authorized scope.

## Establish the comparison

Use the alternatives the user provides and add relevant options that could improve
the outcome. When none are provided, identify viable alternatives yourself. Try to
offer at least three distinct alternatives; when fewer are viable within the agreed
scope, explain the limitation and compare those available.

Identify the criteria that matter to this decision from the conversation and
available evidence. Investigate facts you can retrieve from the project, tools, or
authoritative sources before asking the user. Verify feasibility and changing
capabilities against current evidence. Distinguish facts, assumptions, and unknowns;
cite evidence where it affects the comparison.

## Resolve decisive gaps

A gap is decisive when its answer could plausibly change the preferred option under
the user's priorities. If the goal, constraints, preferences, or evidence leave such
a gap, ask **one question at a time**, starting with the answer that most shapes the
comparison. Explain why it matters and try to offer at least three meaningful
answers when viable, while allowing the user to propose another.

Wait for the answer, update the comparison, and repeat only while decisive gaps
remain. Use conditional scenarios to explain how the answer would affect the
choice, keeping the final recommendation pending. If a decisive fact remains
unavailable, state the limitation and identify the smallest way to resolve it.
Minor unknowns can remain explicit assumptions when they would not change the choice.

## Compare and recommend

Scale the depth to the decision's impact, uncertainty, and cost. Start compact and
expand when needed for the user to assess the tradeoffs. Always include:

- **Pros and cons for every alternative**, tied to the same relevant criteria.
- **A comparison** explaining the decisive differences and tradeoffs between options.
- **A recommendation** identifying the preferred option and why its advantages
  outweigh its disadvantages for this user.

When ready, lead with the recommendation and support it with a compact comparison
table or equivalent clear presentation. Explain why it beats the strongest
alternative and which assumptions or changed conditions would reverse the choice.
Use qualitative judgment unless evidence supports quantitative estimates or scoring.
Conclude when the recommendation is supported and no decisive gap remains; user
acceptance of the recommendation is a separate decision.
