# Product increment interviews

Read this for a new functionality, a product change, or a review that must resolve
product choices. Grilling owns interview rounds; this reference owns product context
and the knowledge needed to propose a consistent next slice. For a product decision
or learning review, use this context guidance with the selected decision goal; apply
the slice sufficiency check only when defining delivery increments.

## Read the affected context

Use [consolidate](../../consolidate/SKILL.md) to resolve configured
authority and the affected functionality, shared rules, decisions, proposals, and
evidence. Read the applicable domain glossary and existing ADRs or technical constraints
through project configuration. For a new feature, use its relevant family and direction
rather than invent an existing contract. Identify missing sources and observation gaps.

Read the material relevant to the intended change and its consequences. Retain existing
identities, vocabulary, and confirmed choices; distinguish intended behavior from actual
delivery. Read [delivery slices](../../consolidate/references/delivery-slices.md)
when increment records already exist or their meanings need clarification.

## Select and clarify the next scope

Establish the next usable result or set of increments the user wants to consider now.
Develop the selection together when the user starts with a broad idea. Keep future
possibilities discoverable as deferred proposals rather than expanding the current
interview. A cross-cutting question belongs now when it materially affects the selected
outcome, scope, acceptance, or prerequisites.

Resolve choices that could change this slice's consistency. Retrieve factual evidence
from available sources; use the user for decisions rather than discoverable facts.
Retain confirmed choices as they settle. Use the canonical writer within existing
documentation authority; unresolved alternatives remain proposals.

## Demonstrate sufficient knowledge

The next step is being able to formulate a consistent vertical slice. Propose closure
with a concise candidate showing:

- the actor's usable end-to-end result and relevant value;
- bounded conditions, audiences, or cases, including what is deferred;
- verifiable acceptance and material exceptions, referencing settled rules;
- known prerequisites, exposure intent where relevant, and consequential uncertainty.

Use this outline to demonstrate sufficient knowledge, without requiring the final
slice record, technical design, or ticket breakdown. Routine implementation choices
can remain for execution; identify gaps that would invalidate the proposed result or
scope. Existing exposure control can be a prerequisite; a new flag can remain proposed
without claiming release authority or observed availability.

The user can accept closure, identify missing knowledge, or choose to explore another
slice. Add another slice only from that choice and apply the same check. On accepted
closure, hand off the selected scope, confirmed choices, canonical references, and
deferred questions for consolidation and selected delivery planning.

## When evidence blocks the slice

First make quick checks against available documentation, code, and observations within
the session. When further evidence is indispensable, propose a bounded investigation:
question, scope, method, expected evidence or output, and completion criterion.
Keep that executable discovery work distinguishable from a product delivery slice.
Carry it into work planning under the existing publication and execution authority.

A smaller slice is preferable when it still provides relevant progress toward the
user's goal. Preserve the deferred uncertainty and its implications. Longer research
uses the discovery handoff rather than keeping the interview open waiting for results.

Return supported findings to canonical knowledge and read them when resuming. Preserve
settled choices and resolve the remaining consequential uncertainty for the selected
slice. Approved hypotheses and experiment outcomes remain distinguishable from policy.
