# Evaluate a Cheaper Decision Ensemble

Use this branch for an explicit cost/reliability comparison or adoption of an
evaluated configuration. Default selection remains independent for every assignment
under `SKILL.md`. The ensemble's final outcome is the unit of reliability in a
comparison; its agent count creates no automatic capability discount.

## Assess the protocol

Identify the decision class and material subtype: product, architecture, code
design, tone and manner, factual truth, or another explicit class. For a mixed
decision, identify every determinant component. Identify the consequences of a wrong
accepted choice, available evidence, independent deliberations, claim verification, judgment, and the bounded
route for unresolved decisions. Preserve the user's risk and authorization gates.

For each role, identify the reasoning it must perform, which mistakes later stages
can detect, and which mistakes could survive into an accepted choice. Include shared
evidence gaps, framing errors, and failures of the verifier or judge. Fresh contexts
isolate deliberations; their errors may still be correlated.

## Choose the combination

Compare an economical ensemble against the individual baseline. Lightweight
deciders, verifiers, and judges are evaluation candidates; assign tiers by role and
observed weaknesses. A stronger judge is an option, not a requirement.

Use a cheaper joint allocation in ordinary work only when relevant confirmation
data supports the final acceptance criteria and comparable coverage for that class
and subtype. Preserve explicit user model constraints. An authorized experiment
may test a provisional configuration; record its untested assumptions and keep its
results distinct from a qualified selection. Verification mechanisms explain how
the protocol could work; evaluate whether they actually catch its failures.

Estimate total cost across deliberation, verification, judgment, bounded
reconsideration, failures, and user escalations. Follow `SKILL.md`'s local pricing
snapshot rules when selecting rates, and record the billing surface and any pricing
uncertainty. Refresh the snapshot only on an explicit user request. Several
inexpensive calls may cost less than one expensive call; compare
their actual token use and final results rather than call count alone.

## Calibrate and dispatch

For a claim of equal reliability, compare configurations on representative decisions
against evidence or an independently established reference, using the same acceptance
criteria. Measure wrong accepted decisions, unresolved or user-escalated decisions,
total cost, and completion time. Evaluate quality at comparable completion coverage
so an ensemble that escalates nearly everything cannot appear equivalent.

Calibrate classes and material subtypes separately. Use executable or source-based
references for objective checks and a frozen, independently labeled rubric for value
or style judgments. A pooled score cannot establish reliability in an untested class;
a mixed decision needs support for each determinant component. Freeze configurations,
prompts, references, and budgets before exposing fresh confirmation cases to the
tested models. Preserve generator seeds and artifact hashes for replay, and retire
exposed cases from future untouched confirmation sets. New local instances support
a prospective-exposure claim, not proof of the provider's training history.

Record the protocol, per-role models and efforts, checks supporting the selection,
evaluation evidence or provisional status, and concrete escalation triggers. Apply
the delegation rules to every dispatch using this plan and the current assignment.
Reassess affected roles when evidence exposes a failure the existing checks cannot
reliably catch. Preserve the protocol's deliberation budget and the user's model
constraints.

**Complete when:** every assignment has an explicit configuration tied to the final
acceptance criteria, and the expected savings and reliability assumptions are
traceable in the decision record.
