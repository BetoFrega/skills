# Product context

Read this when maintaining product direction, business context, shared rules,
vocabulary relationships, or discovery evidence.

## Direction and business context

Record the current audiences, problems, value proposition, objectives, principles,
strategy, and material business constraints appropriate to the product. Distinguish
approved direction from options, hypotheses, and observed results. Preserve authority,
effective timing, evidence, and review conditions where relevant. Objectives express
intended outcomes; evidence records what has actually happened.

Use existing canonical objects and their configured relationships. When changing
direction, follow [decisions](decisions.md) for the accepted choice and rationale, and
identify affected capabilities, rules, proposals, and work. Routine clarification may
fit in the existing object and its native history.

## Shared rules

Keep rules applying across functionalities in one authoritative record and link them
from each affected contract. Give each rule a stable reference, scope, actors,
conditions, expected or prohibited outcomes, exceptions, authority, and effective
timing as applicable. Describe conflicts and precedence explicitly when overlapping
rules affect behavior.

Feature-specific rules belong in [catalogue records](catalogue.md). Promote a rule to
shared authority when its scope warrants it; preserve the original references and
reason for the move. When a shared rule changes, locate all affected functionalities
and work through native relationships or a documented search. Account for each with
a contract update, an unaffected explanation, or an identified gap.

## Vocabulary

Reference the domain glossary for definitions and terminology. Follow the configured
domain conventions and [glossary format](../../domain-modeling/CONTEXT-FORMAT.md) for
approved definition changes; surface unresolved meanings or context boundaries.
Keep product behavior and policy in their product authority, linking
glossary terms to it where useful. A glossary entry alone does not establish a product
permission, invariant, or business commitment.

## Evidence and discovery

Capture the question, hypothesis or raw idea, method, audience and context, source,
observation date, result, limitations, and interpretation appropriate to the evidence.
Distinguish observations from conclusions and estimates. Link evidence to the choices
and functionalities it informs. Preserve inconclusive and contrary results.

Approval to run an experiment is scoped to that experiment. Its observations do not
automatically become permanent policy. Use [proposals](proposals.md) when an idea has
enough shape for an investment discussion; use [catalogue](catalogue.md) when it meets
the proposal admission criteria. Retain its discovery provenance.

Complete when changed context or evidence has verified persistence and links, and
every affected contract or decision has an accounted-for consequence or remaining gap.
