# Luna Decision Pilot — 2026-10-02

The live pilot completed: 24 freshly generated cases, paired individual and ensemble
protocols, and 98 successful Codex calls. Every worker requested `gpt-6-luna` with
`high` effort. The ensemble accepted one wrong decision; the individual accepted
six. Its conditional API-equivalent cost was 3.58 times the individual cost.

These are diagnostic observations. A source-checking defect affected the ensemble,
the sample is small, and neither protocol met the frozen reliability target. This
run does not establish equal confidence with Terra, Sol, or Haiku.

## Scope and frozen protocol

The class was **architecture constraint reasoning**: eight operations and eight
candidate schedules, with dependencies, capacity, retries, deadlines, maintenance
cost, and possible capacity worlds. The required answer was a plan that is feasible
and optimal in every world, or escalation when there is no common optimum.
This finite problem does not measure open-ended architectural quality. The
publication, upload, and image scenario names are fictional; they do not assert
facts about the Tudo Macaé repository or production environment.

Of the 24 cases, 17 permitted an accepted plan and seven required escalation. Three
of those seven contained an explicit human gate and were escalated mechanically by
both protocols, without a model call. There were 21 actual reasoning cases per arm.

The individual protocol used one decider. The ensemble used two isolated deciders,
literal source checks, at most one additional decider, and a judge with no research
tools. Agreement required matching choice, scope, conditions, and determinant
literal claim sets. Different true premises could trigger an additional decider;
this conservative agreement rule has a cost and is not a semantic equivalence test.
All decisions were proposals; none was executed in a product or repository.

The generator, plan, prompts, and CLI transport were frozen before drawing a new
256-bit seed. Decision structure and values varied along with opaque identifiers
and nonces. Exact instances were created locally for this pilot. This resists
memorized-instance reuse but does not prove absence of equivalent reasoning or
templates from provider training.

## Failure analysis and harness correction

Case indices here are zero-based, matching the packet filenames.

The individual accepted infeasible plans in cases 2 and 14. In cases 5, 10, 15,
and 20 it accepted a feasible plan that was optimal in only one capacity world,
although the required routing was escalation because no common optimum existed.

The ensemble's remaining wrong acceptance was case 15. All three deciders chose
the same plan and the judge accepted it. Its score was 99 in both worlds, while
the world-specific minima were 99 and 72. The judge also asserted that every
other plan violated a feasibility rule. The shared mistake survived consensus
and adjudication; independent sessions did not eliminate correlated errors.

The v1 literal checker admitted only the `evidence` array, overlooking the
top-level `required_operations` field that workers could legitimately cite.
Among accepted final reports, 11 claims marked unsupported matched that field
exactly. One other claim was a real model mismatch: the individual report in
case 19 stated a `release_lock` duration of 3 instead of the supplied 2. Its
selected choice was nevertheless correct under the frozen choice scorer.

The checker defect also affected routing in case 12: all three deciders chose
the correct plan, but the judge explicitly escalated because the checker called
the required-operation list unsupported. This explains the one unnecessary
escalation. Its observed outcome remains an escalation. A corrected outcome is
unobserved, and the report does not substitute hypothetical acceptance.

Current source version v2 admits packet metadata to literal checks and schedules
initial work consistently across both arms. Regression checks cover both fixes.
The frozen v1 source, inputs, references, scores, and live reports remain unchanged.
There has been no corrected live rerun. A confirmation study must freeze v2 and
generate fresh untouched cases; this pilot cannot become its own confirmation set.

## Execution and reference audit

There were 98 distinct Codex thread IDs, no missing session IDs, no prompt hash
mismatches, and no recorded tool or research items. All calls completed; none
timed out. Recorded configuration was Luna/high throughout. The CLI was
`codex-cli 0.157.1`; model headers were absent, and requested aliases do not
identify immutable hosted weights.

A separate discrete-time implementation reproduced all 24 private answer sets
without disagreement with the frozen event-sweep reference. This checks the
finite specification, not its realism as a product decision.
